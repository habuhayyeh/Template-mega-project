| D-030 | Two-axis neurologic problem model (signer, 2026-11-15). For every neurologic item, separate **encounter role** from **causal relationship**. Encounter role has only two categories: `PRIMARY` = active problem being evaluated/managed now, or active neurologic disorder independently driving current neurologic assessment/management; `BACKGROUND` = established or suspected neurologic disease/finding not itself treated as a primary current problem. Historical relevance alone does not make a disease primary. Background neurologic problems remain compactly represented in the A/P with disease, residual deficit/course, treatment, and follow-up when available. Separately, causal relation to a current syndrome is `LIKELY`, `POSSIBLE`, or `UNLIKELY`, using the clinically appropriate relation noun (`CONTRIBUTOR`, `ETIOLOGY`, `MECHANISM`, or `CAUSE`). Encounter role and causal strength are independent: a BACKGROUND disease may have a LIKELY causal contribution to a PRIMARY syndrome. When an old disease contributes through a persistent deficit, represent the acting deficit/mechanism (e.g. chronic right-sided weakness from prior L MCA stroke) as the contributor rather than substituting the historical disease label. Do not infer a causal relation, stability, worsening, treatment, or follow-up from historical presence alone. Intentional reuse is allowed: the residual deficit may appear in the current problem's causal reasoning while the underlying disease remains a compact background A/P problem. If the historical disease is itself actively evaluated/managed, it becomes PRIMARY. D-030 supersedes D-015 and D-023 wherever they imply that A/P retention requires current management or that a retained inactive major neurologic diagnosis is therefore a PRIMARY problem; their retention/condensation principles remain only insofar as consistent with this two-axis rule. |
